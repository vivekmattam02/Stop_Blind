\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:data}
\label{sec:protocol}

\paragraph{Data.}
We train on the released R2R-CE training split \citep{krantz2020beyond}: 197,455 eligible decisions
from 3,603 physical trajectories in 61 scenes. For testing in the simulator, we froze a separate set
of 61 \texttt{val\_seen} episodes spanning 53 scenes. These episodes were chosen without reference to
any policy's results. They are the hardest expert path in each scene (the most blocked forward
steps, then the most actions and turns), plus every other trajectory whose released shortest-path
length differs from the simulator's, 12 such trajectories in all. None of them is used in training or in
any held-out set. We split the training data by physical trajectory, so the several instructions
written for one path always fall in the same part. Of the 3,603 eligible trajectories, 2,451 supply
3,072 training decisions. The rest supply one held-out decision each: 256 for checkpoint selection
(the selection set), 640 for the natural set, whose actions keep their usual proportions, and 256 for the balanced set,
with 64 per action.
Scenes are Matterport3D scans \citep{chang2017matterport3d}, used under its terms of use
\citep{matterport2017tos} and rendered in Habitat-Sim.

\paragraph{Model and training.}
For each decision, the model receives the instruction and the last four camera frames, and predicts
one of four action tokens: \textsc{stop}, \textsc{move\_forward}, \textsc{turn\_left} or
\textsc{turn\_right}. A decision is
step $t$ of an expert trajectory, with $3\le t\le 96$, and its frames are the RGB images at steps
$t-3$ to $t$ ($224\times224$ pixels, 90-degree field of view), rendered by replaying the expert's
actions. The policy receives no depth. Training and scoring are therefore teacher-forced, with
every frame taken from the expert's path. The loss is the cross-entropy over the four
action tokens (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}), and the predicted action is the highest-scoring one, with
an exact tie going to the lowest action ID. In the simulator, the frames are the policy's own, and
the first three steps are padded with copies of the first frame. We freeze Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct
\citep{bai2025qwen3vl} (Hugging Face revision \texttt{0c351dd}) and train only a rank-32 adapter
(LoRA) \citep{hu2022lora} on its attention layers. We train with AdamW (learning rate
$2\times10^{-5}$, weight decay $0.01$, gradient clipping at $1.0$, microbatch 1, gradient
accumulation 16) for 192 updates. The only difference between the two treatments is
inverse-frequency weighting, normalized so that the mean sample weight is one. The three seeds,
$20260825$, $20260826$, and $20260827$, change only the adapter's initialization and the order of
examples. Everything else is identical across runs, including the data, the held-out sets, the
model, the optimizer, the selection rule and the thresholds.

\paragraph{Checkpoint selection and the existence gate.}
Each run saves a checkpoint every 32 updates, including the untrained model at update 0. The
selection rule keeps the checkpoint with the lowest mean cross-entropy on the selection set (the
selection loss), and the earlier one on an exact tie. The existence gate, fixed before the first run, asks whether a
model has learned more than how often each action occurs, by comparing it with two baselines that
ignore the input. On the natural set, a model is compared with the frequency-only baseline, which
always predicts the training action frequencies and whose loss is the entropy of those frequencies,
$H=0.9247$ nats (natural-log units). On the balanced set, it is compared with a uniform guess, whose
loss is $\ln 4=1.3863$ and whose accuracy is $0.25$. The existence gate passes only if three conditions
hold. The natural-set loss must fall below $H-0.05$ and the balanced-set loss below $\ln 4-0.05$,
both judged by the upper end of a $95\%$ interval from resampling scenes, and balanced
\textsc{stop} recall must be at least $0.25$.

\paragraph{Simulator evaluation.}
Our pinned Habitat-Sim evaluator \citep{savva2019habitat} reproduces all 778 released
\texttt{val\_seen} expert trajectories and their official metrics. Besides SR and OSR, we report SPL,
success weighted by path length, and SDTW, success weighted by how closely the path follows the
expert's (normalized dynamic time warping, nDTW) \citep{ilharco2019general}. Before any of the six
training runs, we fixed a suite of 14 runs of non-learning policies on the same 61 episodes, 854 episode-runs
in all (Table~\ref{tab:evaluator-controls}). Replaying the expert's trajectory succeeds on $61/61$
episodes with SPL $0.9679$, while random and handcrafted policies each succeed on $3$ of their $305$
episodes, so the evaluator does not force zero success. Learned policies run on the same 61
\texttt{val\_seen} episodes (53 scenes) as the controls. Habitat executes 0.25 m forward moves and
15-degree turns, with a 3 m goal radius and the official 500-step cap. Comparisons are paired
by seed and episode. Intervals come from 10,000 fixed-seed resamples of the 53 scenes, keeping all
episodes and seeds of each sampled scene.

\paragraph{Executing every saved checkpoint.}
We executed all 37 distinct saved checkpoints on the same 61 episodes, all on one GPU type (NVIDIA
L40S): the 30 the rule passed over, the untrained model, and the six selected ones again for
comparison. Each passed-over checkpoint is compared with its run's selected checkpoint, and the
untrained model with all six. A candidate counts as better only if it passes a paired test fixed in
advance (a one-sided exact McNemar test with Tarone's correction for discrete tests
\citep{tarone1990modified}) and then passes it again on 61 new episodes
(Appendix~\ref{app:sweep}).

\paragraph{Verification.}
Every input and output is recorded with its hash. Separate verifier programs reproduce the six training
outcomes, all 366 learned rollouts, and the 61 matched always-forward runs without error. For the
37-checkpoint execution, every count was computed again from the raw episode records, independently
of the preregistered analysis script. All tables and figures are generated from these records.

\paragraph{Preregistration and later additions.}
Only the first run, unweighted at seed 20260825, was fully specified before any result, in a
written protocol whose SHA-256 hash the run records. We added the class-weighted runs after that
run collapsed, the other two seeds after both seed-20260825 runs finished, and the simulator runs
after all six. Each addition was fixed before it ran and kept the same selection rule and gate, and
every seed is reported. The weighting comparison reuses the held-out sets that the first run had
already used, so it is adaptive. The checkpoint-selection framing, the episode-level comparisons
with the controls, and the scene-resampled intervals came after the results. Executing every saved
checkpoint was added after review and preregistered on 9 October 2026, before any unselected
checkpoint was executed or scored.
