\section{Reproduction details}
\label{app:repro}

\paragraph{Backbone and adapter.}
\texttt{Qwen/Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct} at Hugging Face revision
\texttt{0c351dd01ed87e9c1b53cbc748cba10e6187ff3b}, run through its own processor and visual pathway.
The LoRA adapter has rank 32, $\alpha=64$ and dropout 0, on the query, key, value and output
projections of each of the 36 language-model attention blocks, for $30{,}670{,}848$ trainable parameters. Every other weight
stays frozen in BF16. The adapter and optimizer state use 32-bit floats.

\paragraph{Decision sampling.}
Each physical trajectory keeps one of its instructions, chosen by a seeded hash. A decision at step
$t$ is eligible when $3\le t\le 96$, so no training or held-out history needs padding. The
selection, natural-outcome and balanced partitions take one decision per trajectory by seeded hash,
the balanced one drawing 64 per action from distinct trajectories. Training takes one decision from
each of its 2,451 trajectories and 621 more from the same trajectories, again by seeded hash, and
visits them once in a seeded order.

\paragraph{Class weighting and selection.}
The weighted arm computes one weight per action from the $3{,}072$ training decisions,
$w_a = N/(4 n_a)$, so that the mean weight over those decisions is one: \textsc{stop} $10.82$
($n=71$), \textsc{move\_forward} $0.379$ ($2{,}024$), \textsc{turn\_left} $1.430$ ($537$) and
\textsc{turn\_right} $1.745$ ($440$). Each decision contributes $w_y(-\log p_y)$ for its expert action
$y$. The $16$ decisions of an update are each divided by $16$ and accumulated, with no
renormalization of the weights within an update. The weights act on training only: checkpoint
selection and every cross-entropy reported here use the unweighted four-way loss in both arms.

\paragraph{Prompt, history and action tokens.}
A system message lists the four action IDs and asks for an answer of the form ``Action ID: $N$''.
The user message holds the four frames in time order, each preceded by a label such as
``Frame t-1 (previous action: 1=MOVE\_FORWARD).'', and ends with the instruction and ``Choose the
next official action now.'' The target ``Action ID: $N$'' tokenizes to four shared tokens (ids 2512,
3034, 25, 220) and one digit token: 15 for \textsc{stop} ($N=0$), 16 for \textsc{move\_forward}
($N=1$), 17 for \textsc{turn\_left} ($N=2$) and 18 for \textsc{turn\_right} ($N=3$). Only the digit
position is supervised, and every score compares the four digit tokens at that one position. In
closed loop, a frame that does not yet exist is replaced by a copy of the initial observation and
labeled as such, with no previous action. Training never sees such a padded history, and six of the
seven weighted \textsc{stop} presses fall on step 1, where it occurs, so those early stops are
confounded with this untrained input.

\paragraph{Runtime.}
Training ran on one NVIDIA GB10 GPU (aarch64) and the first closed-loop evaluation on the same
aarch64 platform, with Habitat-Sim 0.3.3. All 37 policies were later executed and scored on NVIDIA
L40S GPUs (x86-64, Appendix~\ref{app:sweep}). Because some stored decisions are exact BF16 ties (Appendix~\ref{app:ties}), another GPU model could
change individual actions.

\paragraph{Protocol records.}
Each training run records the SHA-256 hash of every protocol file it followed. The protocol for the
two later seeds was edited after their runs finished, to mark it completed and add a closing note.
No rule, seed or threshold changed, and those four runs record the hash of the version they followed.
