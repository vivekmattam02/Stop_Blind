\section{Conclusion}

We studied how checkpoints are selected when a VLM is fine-tuned to navigate, a task whose success
depends on stopping at the goal. In all three unweighted runs, the teacher-forced validation loss
selects the same checkpoint, and that checkpoint never predicts \textsc{stop}. In the simulator,
two of those checkpoints match the always-forward baseline in every episode, the third differs in
one, and none stops. The averaged loss gives no warning, because \textsc{stop} is only 3 of the 256
decisions it averages. Balanced \textsc{stop} recall ($0/64$ without weighting) and the existence
gate do warn. Executing all 37 distinct saved checkpoints yields a single success, by the untrained
model in one episode, and no checkpoint was shown to navigate better than the selected one. We
recommend reporting recall on the action that defines success alongside any validation loss used
to select checkpoints.
