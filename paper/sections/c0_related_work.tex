\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Rare actions in navigation imitation losses.} The closest prior work concerns rare
decisions under an unweighted imitation loss. In embodied question answering,
\citet{wijmans2019eqa} warned that policies that repeat the previous action ``achieve very
high validation accuracy for action prediction but miss every transition between actions''. The
R2R-CE reference baseline, VLN-CE (VLN in Continuous Environments), trains with their inflection weighting, which upweights steps where the
expert's action changes \citep{krantz2020beyond}. \citet{xiang2020l2stop} argued that
\textsc{stop}, which occurs once per episode, ``will receive less attention if we treat all actions
equally'', and added a dedicated stopping head. NaVILA, a VLM fine-tuned for R2R-CE, rebalances its
labels, in particular because \textsc{stop} is underrepresented. Removing the rebalancing lowers success on
\texttt{val\_unseen}, the split of unseen buildings that this study never opened, from $49.7$ to
$30.0$ \citep{cheng2025navila}. A small VLM fine-tuned for aerial navigation was ``critically
affected by a severe action imbalance in the training data'' \citep{tamazyan2025aerial}. On the
discrete navigation graph, agents in four systems pass the goal without stopping
\citep{zhao2023mindthegap}, and a case study of one agent finds that its errors reflect a forward
bias learned in training \citep{yang2023behavioral}. These works change training or analyze trained
agents. We instead measure which checkpoint plain cross-entropy selects for a VLM policy in continuous
R2R-CE, where success depends on the rare \textsc{stop} action.

\paragraph{Offline metrics and closed-loop competence.} In driving, offline prediction error need
not correlate with driving quality \citep{codevilla2018offline}, and open-loop metrics on a modern
planning benchmark favor planners that nearly ignore their input \citep{dauner2023parting}. In
manipulation, checkpoints selected on validation data are $10$--$100\%$ worse than those selected by
rollout \citep{mandlekar2021robomimic}, and choosing a trained policy's input-modality mask by
lowest validation loss picked a mask with zero success in all three seeds \citep{hao2023masked}.
Behavior-cloned policies can reach near-zero held-out error by relying on spurious cues that fail at
deployment \citep{dehaan2019causal}, including collapse toward a dominant action
\citep{codevilla2019exploring}. Two results narrow the gap: better-chosen validation data improve
the correlation between offline and online scores \citep{codevilla2018offline}, and under log-loss
with a discrete action vocabulary, compounding error grows at most polynomially with the horizon
\citep{foster2024behavior}. Our setting differs from these studies in two ways: success in
navigation hinges on one rare action, and the offline metric we test is plain cross-entropy used to
select checkpoints.

\paragraph{Checkpoint selection in R2R-CE.} R2R-CE papers that state a selection rule select on
closed-loop \texttt{val\_unseen} performance \citep{krantz2020beyond}. NaVILA trains for one epoch
and states no selection rule \citep{cheng2025navila}. We found no R2R-CE paper that selects
checkpoints by teacher-forced loss. We select by it deliberately, execute every checkpoint it chose
among, and leave \texttt{val\_unseen} sealed. Appendix~\ref{app:related} discusses this work in
more detail.
